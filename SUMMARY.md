# sanding-wear-tracker: summary

This simulation study asks whether a sanding process model can keep track of two unknowns at once, a pad stiffness that is fixed but uncertain and an abrasive sharpness that drops every pass, and whether that still works when the simulated process contains effects the tracker does not model (a foam pad, a Preston pressure exponent, force errors, a two-stage wear law, scanner effects). It was motivated by GrayMatter Robotics' post *[World Models for Manufacturing Processes](https://factory.graymatter-robotics.com/world-models-for-manufacturing-processes/)*, but it is independent work and uses no GrayMatter data or code. A particle filter that re-estimates stiffness, force dependence, effectiveness and a drifting wear rate from each post-pass scan predicted the next pass far better than a nominal model or a model calibrated once, better than a baseline that refits every scan when the process contained unmodelled effects (about as well when it did not), and chose when to change the abrasive more accurately than any of them; under the unmodelled effects its 90% intervals were too narrow (82% coverage), its parameters became effective rather than true values, and effects that reshape the removal map beyond a linear pad (abrasive wearing ring by ring) left errors several times an oracle's. Nothing was tested on physical parts.

![Prediction error per pass](figures/fig3_rmse_per_pass.png)

**Key number:** in the world with unmodelled effects, over 100 simulated hidden truths, the particle filter changed the abrasive within one pass of the right time in 92 of 96 runs whose wear crossed the threshold (refitting every scan: 54; waiting for the scans to show it: 75), and its median pass-20 removal-map error was 0.250 µm against 0.303 µm for refitting every scan and 0.110 µm for an oracle that knows the true physics and state.
